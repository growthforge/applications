Application for the AI & Business Solutions Graduate role

A small pilot that keeps every complaint date in view

Shanly Homes publishes a timetable for formal complaints: replies within 5, 10, 30 and 56 days, then at least every 28. I designed a small Microsoft 365 tool around it. It works out every date as soon as a complaint arrives, reminds the team before each one falls due, and has Copilot draft the letters for a person to approve.

The date rules are built and tested here. The SharePoint, Teams and Power Automate parts are designed and checked against Microsoft's published formats, but nothing has been installed in a live Microsoft 365 tenant.

Why this process

Public rules, easy to check

I chose this process because its rules are public and precise enough to test against, not because anything needs fixing.

A formal complaint can be raised for two years from reservation or legal completion, whichever is later, and from day 56 a customer may refer it to the New Homes Ombudsman Service.

Three codes apply at once, chosen by the reservation date: the Consumer Code for Home Builders before 2 August 2023, version 1 of the New Homes Quality Code from 2 August 2023 to 1 March 2026, and version 2 from 2 March 2026. The register suggests the right one and a person confirms it.

How it would run

  1. A complaint arrives in the shared mailboxOutlook
  2. A coordinator confirms the type and dates on a Teams cardPower Automate and Teams
  3. The register works out every due date by formulaSharePoint list
  4. A daily reminder goes out two working days before each datePower Automate and Teams
  5. Copilot drafts the letter; a person approves and sends itCopilot in Outlook or Copilot Chat, Approvals

Questions I would expect from IT

  • Licensing: standard connectors only (Outlook, SharePoint, Teams, Approvals). The automated AI step is a later option, after a licensing check.
  • Ownership: a service account with at least two co-owners, in a solution outside the default environment.
  • Failure: run-failure alerts, plus a short daily "clock ran" message so a missed day is obvious.
  • Personal data: stays in the tenant; the register is restricted to the Complaints Team; retention agreed with your data protection lead, and a DPIA before any automated AI step.
  • Upkeep and exit: a yearly bank-holiday refresh from GOV.UK; turn the flows off and the team carries on by hand.

The build pack and the handover note

How I went about it

Rules first, then AI where it helps

  • The rules became tests before anything else: Shanly Homes' own examples, Easter, Christmas, the code boundaries and the two-year window. Every test passes.
  • Dates by formula: due dates are SharePoint formulas anyone can read; AI only reads emails and drafts letters.
  • Prompts checked before suggesting them: a smoke test on illustrative emails written separately from the prompt. The first run failed because the prompt left out three fields; both runs are in the build pack.
  • Agent-led build, checked at every step: as on client work, I directed AI coding agents (Claude Code) and made the checks part of the build, so every date rule has its own test and every Microsoft file is validated automatically before anyone relies on it. The results are in the build pack.
  • If you choose this, I would start by spending time with the Complaints Team to see how complaints are handled today, then trial it alongside the current process.
