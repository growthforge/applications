The rules, in plain English

Every rule is written down so ATCs and control partners can check it. The page runs on the same file you can download: change the file and the desk changes.

The tiers follow the FCA's good-practice note of 19 October 2026: existing rules apply to AI, and firms do well with simpler risk tiers that add testing, guardrails and monitoring as risk rises. [S10]

Route to delivery: the first rule that matches wins

  1. Use what already exists when a firm-wide programme may already cover it. No second version of a firm-wide build.
  2. Use what already exists when a tool the firm already owns may do it. Check the AI features of what the firm already owns first.
  3. Process first when the first fix is process: nobody owns the rule, or inputs arrive in different shapes. Agree the owner, rule or input standard, then re-run the desk.
  4. External partner or vendor product when the capability is not available in-house. Usually a purchase through the Buy workstream, with vendor due diligence.
  5. Technology when it needs core platform integration or writes to a system of record. Escalated demand goes to Forward Deployed Engineering.
  6. Process automation team when it is deterministic work on structured data. A flow, not a model.
  7. Business users when one person or one team, all users licensed, Microsoft 365 only, tier 1 or 2, and the output stays with the user or team. A tested pattern in users' own hands.
  8. AI Business Partner when anything else that is an AI build. The Partner builds it and hands it to a Fighter Pilot.

Control tier: set by data and destination, never by the model

  • Tier 3: full control review. Client, personal or market-sensitive data (or data nobody has labelled yet), or output that goes to the board, auditors, regulators or clients, or into a system of record. Technology, AI Governance, Information Security and Compliance or Risk review before the build; a named approver for every output; logging on.
  • Tier 2: checklist review. Confidential internal data, or output that feeds a team document or decision. Technology and the data owner sign a short checklist before the pilot.
  • Tier 1: notify only. Internal, non-sensitive data used in the person's own work. A register entry on a pre-approved pattern; no review meeting.

Added on top of the tier, where they apply

  • DPIA screening with the Data Protection Officer before any personal data is processed.
  • Compliance confirms information-barrier and insider-list controls (UK MAR) before build.
  • The data owner labels the source data first; until then it is treated as tier 3.
  • Oversharing check on the SharePoint sites the agent will ground on: permissions and sensitivity labels reviewed first.
  • Financial promotion sign-off where the output is marketing; a Consumer Duty check where retail clients will see it.
  • The accountable owner signs the final version, and that version is kept for the record.
  • An accountable Senior Manager is named in the register (SM&CR).

Worth pursuing: transparent arithmetic

Value is hours at stake (under 5 hours a month scores 1; under 20, 2; under 80, 3; more, 4) plus reach (one person 1 up to most of the firm 4) plus the benefit chosen (quality, risk or revenue 1, and quality or risk at tier 3 a further 1).

Effort is the connection (Microsoft 365 only 1; an allowed connector 2; a premium connector or a bought product 3; core platforms 4) plus review load (tier 2 adds 1, tier 3 adds 2) plus 1 where users lack Copilot licences, minus 1 where a pattern already exists.

Effort of 6 or more: start with a smaller first slice. Value at least effort plus 3: pursue now. At least effort plus 1: worth a pilot. Otherwise it is logged and rechecked.

The first 90 days in a unit, with one idea carried end to end

Month-end variance commentary is the thread: it starts as a process question, becomes an AI Business Partner build, and leaves a pattern behind for the next unit. Variance analysis and monthly packs appear in the firm's own FP&A adverts; everything else here is illustrative. [S6] The line under each step is what runs alongside it in the unit.

Illustrative plan. Steps and timings would be agreed with the unit lead.
  1. Weeks 1 and 2

    Register and triage

    The desk goes live beside the Use Case Library. Variance commentary comes out as process first: the reasons behind each variance arrive in different forms. A time log on the next close sets the baseline.

    The ATC and I triage the existing ideas and any personal builds already in use, without blame.

  2. Weeks 3 and 4

    Standardise the input

    The FP&A lead and budget holders agree a short reason template captured with the figures. The desk is re-run: AI Business Partner, tier 2. The benefit owner signs a value case in hours.

    The first self-serve pattern, a weekly digest prompt, goes into the Prompt Library with a Fighter Pilot as owner.

  3. Weeks 5 and 6

    Build with a Fighter Pilot

    A Copilot Studio agent and a Power Automate flow draft commentary from the agreed reasons and the period's figures. The flow checks every figure in a draft against the source extract. It is built as a solution in a managed environment, with the tier 2 checklist signed.

    Test set: the commentary from the last three closes, scored on the agreed rubric.

  4. Weeks 7 and 8

    Parallel run, with a kill criterion

    The pilot runs alongside the normal process for one close. Analysts edit and approve every line, and the time log is repeated. If verified hours fall below the floor agreed with the FP&A lead, or an unchecked figure reaches a draft, it stops for rework.

    Adoption: short sessions built on the unit's own examples, run by the Fighter Pilot.

  5. Weeks 9 and 10

    Go live and hand over

    The ATC and the FP&A lead agree go-live. The Fighter Pilot becomes owner, connections move to a service account, and the benefit owner signs the first verified hours.

    The ATC takes the monthly benefit check and the register into the monthly ATC catch-up.

  6. Weeks 11 and 12

    Publish, then rotate

    The pattern record goes into the Use Case Library: problem shape, controls, measure and lessons, written so another unit can use it without me.

    The next unit inherits the desk, the register and the pattern.

How the desk would run inside Microsoft 365

Your advert names Copilot Studio and Power Automate as today's toolkit and calls the strategy platform-agnostic. The firm has also announced Claude and Cowork for staff, and its engineering adverts describe its own agentic workspace for citizen developers and a central MCP server for governed access to enterprise data. So the desk names approved platforms by zone and data class, not one vendor. [S1] [S2] [S5]

The three delivery zones are Microsoft's own: citizen development, partnered development with a coach and IT approval to publish, and professional development. The intake agent follows Microsoft's canonical pattern for an intake and routing agent: collect the information, apply predefined routing logic, hand over, and never improvise a resolution. [S7] [S8]

AI drafts and matches

It summarises the idea, suggests answers, finds similar patterns and drafts the pattern record. It never sets the route or the tier.

Rules route

Route, tier, priority and controls come from the rules file, so every decision can be explained and the same idea always gets the same first view.

People decide

The colleague confirms the summary, the Partner confirms or overrides the route, control partners approve, and the benefit owner signs the hours.

Hours at stake are not hours saved

The advert names four measures: hours saved, quality improvement scores, risk reduction percentages and revenue growth. Each needs a baseline taken before anything is built, and someone other than the builder to confirm it. [S1]

Reported time savings are weak evidence on their own. The UK government's Copilot evaluation did not find evidence that time savings have led to improved productivity, and Microsoft calls its own assisted-hours figures broad approximations. The Academy already separates adoption, a prerequisite, from value, the goal; the desk does the same, tracking routine use apart from the four measures below. [S11] [S9] [S4]

Where I have done parts of this

None of this was at an asset manager; these are the closest parallels.

  • A tool the team runs itself

    At MTest Labs, a software-testing firm, I built an internal tool with a staged, multi-agent flow grounded in the firm's own test-case library. Test design fell from an 8-hour day, or 2 to 3 days on larger projects, to 1 to 3 supervised hours. A tester approves every case, and I trained the team to run it.

  • Routing and triage redesigned before automating

    At Bnk To The Future, a regulated investment platform, I redesigned case routing, triage and escalation across 100+ cases, with ~25% faster turnaround, and prepared the monthly management and risk-committee reporting.

  • Low-risk steps run alone, consequential ones stay supervised

    At Atelier di Venice I automated dozens of workflows across operations, marketing and finance, from first enquiry to delivery, with an autonomy boundary that keeps client and financial steps under human review.

  • Finance reporting, measured

    At Kuruvella & Co. I built Excel dashboards and VBA macros that cut report preparation time by ~35% across 30+ SME client entities.

Toolkit in use: Copilot Studio, Power Automate, Microsoft 365, Claude, and n8n for over a year. Certified in Microsoft Azure AI Fundamentals (AI-900).
