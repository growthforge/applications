One

The one page that goes back to the client

Plain English, one side of A4. The requirements and the tests sit behind it.

Marlowe Row Maintenance: your billing, from Salesforce to Xero

What we heard, what we will set up, what you decide.

What we heard

  • Owen types every won job into Xero by hand: about six hundred invoices a month, with a pile at month end.
  • Planned maintenance is billed monthly in advance, with a price rise each April. Repairs are billed when the job is finished, sometimes with a deposit first.
  • Nothing should reach a customer without your finance manager seeing it, and they are away in the last week of every month.
  • Customers pay in parts and dispute invoices, the sales team cannot see any of it, and some customers appear twice in Xero. Billing anyone twice would be the worst outcome.

What we will set up

  • A draft invoice appears in Xero when a repair is finished or a monthly invoice falls due, with your account code, VAT treatment, Region, the customer's PO number and payment terms already filled in.
  • Your finance manager approves every draft in Xero, as now. A named person covers when they are away, and a warning goes out the day before the books close if any invoice is still waiting.
  • If an amount changes after an invoice has gone out, nothing is billed twice: your finance manager gets a task and decides in Xero.
  • Payments, part payments, credit notes and voids show against the job in Salesforce for the sales team, who do not see technical errors.
  • Anything unusual, such as a possible double customer, stops and asks. Failures go on a list, with one email each morning only if there were any.

What you decide, and who decides

  1. Where should the monthly billing schedule for a contract live? We suggest a billing schedule in Salesforce, created when a contract is won. (G-01: your finance manager and us)
  2. What counts as a finished repair, and where is the final amount entered? We suggest a completion date and a final amount, entered by operations. (G-02: the managing director and your finance manager)
  3. Which day is each monthly invoice dated, and which April is the first price rise? We suggest each contract's own day, and the first April after the contract starts. (G-17: your finance manager)
  4. Which work is zero rated, exempt or reverse charge? We suggest standard VAT unless your finance manager says otherwise, and reverse charge held for review. Please check with your accountant. (G-05: your finance manager and the accountant)
  5. Which customers must quote a purchase order, and who receives the invoice? We suggest a flag on customers who need a PO, a billing contact for managing agents, and invoices held until a needed PO arrives. (G-06: your finance manager)
  6. Which month do you stop typing, and who links the existing customers? We suggest a month boundary after a rehearsal, with your finance manager deciding each double. (G-09: your finance manager)

First, is Owen your finance manager, or someone who enters invoices for them? It changes who approves. 12 more questions are in the full list.

How we will check it

Everything is built in a Salesforce sandbox linked to a Xero demo company. You can run 19 of the 25 checks yourselves; we run the other 6 while you watch. We rehearse a month end before anything touches your live Xero.

What it will not do yet

The one customer who pays in euros stays manual. Chasing late payers is a separate conversation.

Three

Questions only the client can answer

The call left 18 things open. Each has what I would suggest in the meantime, who decides, and what it holds up. The first six change the design; the rest can wait for an answer without stopping the build.

  1. G-01

    Where does the monthly billing schedule live?

    Why it matters. A won deal is a single event. A 12 or 24 month contract needs somewhere that holds the start date, the term and the monthly amount, and today the contract is a PDF and the start date is in the notes. This is the biggest design decision in the project.

    What I would suggest. Create a billing schedule in Salesforce when a contract is won, and let it generate the monthly drafts. Whether Xero repeating invoices could carry it instead is something to check before we commit.

    Decided by Finance manager and usBlocks REQ-03From

  2. G-02

    What is a completed repair, where does the job live, and where does the final amount come from?

    Why it matters. Repairs are invoiced on completion for a final amount that differs from the quote. That is a job, not a sales stage. Whether jobs stay as opportunities or become something else (for example Field Service work orders) changes the build.

    What I would suggest. A completion date and a final amount entered by operations on the record, for now.

    Decided by MD and finance managerBlocks REQ-01, REQ-02From

  3. G-17

    Which day is each monthly invoice dated, and which April is the first price rise?

    Why it matters. The finance manager described a pile of planned maintenance invoices all at once at month end, which suggests one run rather than each contract's own day. The rise is "whatever the index says" each April, so a contract that starts in March could be uplifted a month later or could wait a year.

    What I would suggest. Date each invoice on the contract's own day (the rule the checks use) and apply the first rise in the first April after the contract starts, until the client says otherwise. A single run day is simpler and the checks would follow.

    Decided by Finance managerBlocks REQ-03From

  4. G-05

    Which work is zero rated, exempt or reverse charge, and who decides?

    Why it matters. The wrong VAT on an invoice is expensive to unpick. Reverse charge was mentioned and has not been confirmed. Reverse charge on construction work can also sit on the purchase bill rather than the sales invoice, depending on who is supplying whom, so the accountant must say which side it touches.

    What I would suggest. Standard unless finance sets another treatment. Reverse charge is held for review. Please confirm with your accountant.

    Decided by Finance manager and the accountantBlocks REQ-05From

  5. G-06

    Which customers must quote a purchase order, and who receives the invoice?

    Why it matters. Housing associations return invoices without a PO, so an invoice for them should be held rather than sent. The invoice goes to the managing agent, not the landlord who is the customer in Salesforce, and it is not yet clear who the contact is in Xero when the agent is billed, which affects the aged debt report.

    What I would suggest. A PO number on the job and a requires-a-PO flag on the customer. A billing contact on the customer, with the paying party kept as the Xero contact until finance decides otherwise. An invoice for a customer that needs a PO is held if there is none.

    Decided by Finance managerBlocks REQ-07From

  6. G-09

    Which month do we stop typing, and who links the existing customers?

    Why it matters. About 400 customers are already in Xero, around 30 of them doubles. Starting without linking them creates more doubles.

    What I would suggest. Start at a month boundary after a sandbox rehearsal. We produce a matching report; finance decides each double.

    Decided by Finance managerBlocks REQ-15, REQ-18From

  7. G-16

    Who is Owen, and who enters the invoices as against who approves them?

    Why it matters. Owen is named as the person who types invoices into Xero, as the person who knows when a repair is done and as the VAT expert. The finance manager is the one who wants to see every invoice before it goes out. The call never says they are the same person. If they are two people, the person who enters and the person who approves are separate, and the cover when the finance manager is away changes. If they are one person, nobody else checks the invoice.

    What I would suggest. Treat Owen as the person who enters and the finance manager as the approver until told otherwise, and set the permissions so that either arrangement can be used.

    Who approves invoices in the last week of the month?

    Why it matters. The finance manager is away that week. Without a named deputy, drafts wait.

    What I would suggest. A named deputy with approval rights in Xero.

  9. G-14

    When an amount changes after an invoice has gone out, who is told and what do they do?

    Why it matters. The finance manager wants to be told and to deal with it in Xero. Nobody has said whether the deal in Salesforce should be locked, or what happens once the invoice has been paid.

    What I would suggest. Approve one at a time while the system is new. After the rehearsal, consider approving the identical monthly invoices as a batch and keep one-by-one approval for repairs.

That needs a view on the customer, not only on each invoice, and it has to decide what to do with debt from before the start month.

    What I would suggest. Refresh hourly and show 'last updated' so nobody is misled. Behind means any invoice overdue, shown on the customer as a count and an amount. Debt from before the start month is loaded as one opening balance, or left out until finance decides.

The requirements behind the one page

20 requirements, each traced to lines of the call. Where I had to assume something, it says so and points to the question that will settle it. Open a requirement for the rule and its Given, When, Then.

Show
  1. REQ-01Repairs are invoiced when the job is completedMustMy assumption

    Repairs are invoiced when the job is finished, for the final amount, not when the deal is won.

    The rule. One DRAFT invoice is created in Xero for the final amount of the job, not the quote.

    • Given a repair opportunity is Closed Won and the job is not complete when nothing changes then no invoice is created.
    • Given the job is marked complete with a final amount when the sync runs then one DRAFT invoice for the final amount exists in Xero.

    From To confirm: G-02Tested by TC-01

  2. REQ-02Deposits on large repairsShouldMy assumption

    Large repairs can be billed as a deposit first (usually half, set per job) and a balance on completion.

    The rule. The deposit is a share of the quote and is invoiced when the deal is won. The balance is the final amount less the deposit and is invoiced on completion, so the two add up to the final amount to the penny. If the final amount is below the deposit, finance is told that a credit is due.

    • Given a repair of 1,001.01 with a 50 percent deposit when the invoices are created then the deposit is 500.51 and the balance is 500.50.
    • Given a repair quoted at 1,001.01 with a 50 percent deposit, completed at a final amount of 1,200 when the balance invoice is created then the deposit is 500.51 and the balance is 699.49, which add up to the final amount.
    • Given a repair quoted at 1,000 with a 50 percent deposit, completed at a final amount of 400 when the balance is worked out then the balance is minus 100 and finance is told that a credit is due.

    From To confirm: G-02Tested by TC-02

  3. REQ-03Monthly invoices for planned maintenanceMustMy assumption

    Planned maintenance contracts are invoiced monthly in advance for the whole term, with the April price rise applied.

    The rule. A billing schedule is created at Closed Won. One DRAFT invoice per month is created in advance, from the start date, for the term, on the contract's own day of the month (pending G-17). The annual April uplift is applied from the first April after the contract starts. Every invoice has its own code: the job number, then the billing month.

    • Given a 12 month contract starting 31 January at 500 a month with a 3.4 percent uplift from April when the schedule is created then the dates are 31 Jan, 28 Feb, 31 Mar, 30 Apr and so on, and the amount is 517 from April.

    From To confirm: G-01, G-03, G-17Tested by TC-03

  4. REQ-04Account code by type of workMustSaid on the call

    The right account code is filled in for you.

    The rule. Planned maintenance lines carry account 200 and repairs 210. Finance can change a code before approving.

    • Given a planned maintenance line when the invoice is drafted then the account code is 200.

    From Tested by TC-04

  5. REQ-05VAT treatmentMustMy assumption

    The right VAT is set on each invoice, and anything unusual is held for finance to decide.

    The rule. Each job has a VAT treatment: Standard, Zero rated, Exempt or No VAT, mapped to the Xero tax types OUTPUT2, ZERORATEDOUTPUT, EXEMPTOUTPUT and NONE. It defaults to Standard. Anything marked reverse charge is held for review and never guessed.

    • Given a job with no VAT treatment when the invoice is drafted then the tax type is OUTPUT2.
    • Given a job marked reverse charge when the sync runs then no invoice is created and finance is asked.

    From To confirm: G-05Tested by TC-05

  6. REQ-06Regional splitMustSaid on the call

    Income is split by North and South for the board pack.

    The rule. Every line carries the Region of the customer (North or South) as the Xero tracking category. A customer with no Region is held, not guessed.

    • Given a South customer when the invoice is drafted then the Region tracking option is South.
    • Given a customer with no Region when the sync runs then no invoice is created and the reason is logged.

    From To confirm: G-10Tested by TC-06

  7. REQ-07Purchase order number, and who is billedMustSaid on the call

    Invoices show the customer's PO number and go to the right person, and an invoice for a customer who needs a PO is held if there is none.

    The rule. When the customer quotes a purchase order, the invoice Reference starts with it, followed by the invoice's own code in brackets. A customer marked as needing a PO (the housing associations) with no PO on the job is held, not drafted, because they send the invoice straight back. The invoice goes to the billing contact when there is one, otherwise to the customer.

    • Given a job with PO HA-7781 and job number OPP-2210 when the invoice is drafted then the Reference reads PO HA-7781 (OPP-2210:FINAL).
    • Given a housing association that needs a PO and a job with none when the sync runs then no invoice is created and finance is asked for the PO.

    From To confirm: G-06Tested by TC-07

  8. REQ-08Payment terms by customerShouldSaid on the call

    Each customer's own payment terms are used.

    The rule. The due date is the invoice date plus the customer's terms: 30 days unless the customer has 45 or 60.

    • Given a customer on 45 day terms when an invoice is dated 1 February 2027 then the due date is 18 March 2027.

    From To confirm: G-07Tested by TC-08

  9. REQ-09Finance approves every invoiceMustSaid on the call

    Nothing goes to a customer without finance seeing it.

    The rule. The sync only ever creates DRAFT invoices and never approves one. Approval happens in Xero by the finance manager.

    • Given any invoice created by the sync when it appears in Xero then its status is DRAFT.
    • Given the sync tries to approve an invoice when it runs then it is refused.

    From To confirm: G-15, G-16Tested by TC-09, TC-10

  10. REQ-10Cover when the finance manager is awayMustMy assumption

    Someone else can approve when the finance manager is off.

    The rule. A named deputy has approval rights in Xero. If the finance manager is away and no deputy is named, finance is told before drafts pile up.

    • Given the finance manager is away and a deputy is named when an invoice needs approval then the deputy is the approver.
    • Given the finance manager is away and no deputy is named when an invoice needs approval then a warning is raised.

    From To confirm: G-04, G-16Tested by TC-11

  11. REQ-11Drafts appear by the end of the working dayShouldMy assumption

    A draft invoice appears by the end of the working day it was triggered, as the managing director asked.

    The rule. A draft invoice appears by 17:00 on the day of the trigger, or by 17:00 on the next working day if the trigger falls outside working hours (09:00 to 17:00, Monday to Friday, bank holidays excluded). If the client wants a tighter target, the number changes and this check follows.

    • Given a trigger at 11:00 on a Wednesday when the draft is created at 16:00 the same day then the target is met.
    • Given a trigger at 18:00 on a Friday when the draft is created at 09:30 on the Monday then the target is met.
    • Given a trigger at 11:00 on a Wednesday when the draft is created at 09:00 on the Thursday then the target is missed.

    From To confirm: G-13Tested by TC-12

  12. REQ-12Changes after an invoice existsMustMy assumption

    If the amount changes after an invoice has gone out, finance is told and no second invoice is made.

    The rule. While the invoice is a draft, the draft is updated. After that the deal is locked in Salesforce, no second invoice is ever created, and a task goes to finance. If the invoice has been paid, finance raises a credit note in Xero.

    • Given an invoice is AUTHORISED when someone changes the deal amount then a task is created for finance and no second invoice is made.

    From To confirm: G-14Tested by TC-13

  13. REQ-13Payment status in SalesforceMustMy assumption

    Sales can see whether a customer has paid, including part payments, before sending a new quote.

    The rule. Salesforce shows Unpaid, Part paid, Paid or Overdue for each invoice, worked out from the amounts paid and due (Xero shows an invoice as approved and awaiting payment even when part paid). It says when it was last updated, because payments appear only after bank matching.

    • Given an invoice of 1,200 with 600 paid and not yet due when the status is worked out then it is Part paid.

    From To confirm: G-08Tested by TC-14

  14. REQ-14Credit notes and voided invoices are visibleShouldSaid on the call

    Sales can see credit notes and voids against the job.

    The rule. Credit notes and voids raised in Xero show against the job in Salesforce. A void on a won deal with no replacement creates a task for finance.

    • Given an invoice of 1,200 with a 200 credit note allocated when the invoice is shown then the net is 1,000.

    From Tested by TC-15, TC-16

  15. REQ-15No duplicate customers, no duplicate invoicesMustSaid on the call

    The system stops and asks rather than guess, and never bills twice.

    The rule. The customer is matched by the stored Xero ID, then by exact email. If several customers match, or a name is only similar, the invoice is held for finance to choose. A retry never creates a second invoice: every invoice has its own code (the job number, then which invoice it is: the billing month, the deposit or the balance), written at the end of the Reference. Before anything is created, Xero is searched for exactly that code. A deposit does not stand in for a balance, and one month does not stand in for the next.

    • Given a customer whose name is similar to an existing Xero customer when the invoice is drafted then it is held for finance.
    • Given a retry after a timeout where Xero already holds the invoice when the sync runs again then the existing invoice is adopted and no second one is created.
    • Given a job whose deposit invoice is already in Xero when the balance invoice is created then it is created as a separate invoice and the deposit is not adopted in its place.

    From To confirm: G-09Tested by TC-17, TC-18

  16. REQ-16A list of failures and a morning emailMustSaid on the call

    Finance finds out about a failure the next morning, never at month end.

    The rule. Every attempt is logged (record, direction, result, message, time). Finance has a list of failures. One email goes each morning if anything failed in the last 24 hours, and none when nothing did.

    • Given two failures in the last 24 hours when it is 8am then one email is sent with a count of two.

    From Tested by TC-19

  17. REQ-17Who sees whatMustSaid on the call

    Sales see payment status but never the technical errors.

    The rule. Finance sees payment status, the log and can re-run a failed sync. Sales sees payment status only.

    • Given a sales user when they open an invoice then they see the payment status but not the log.

    From Tested by TC-20

  18. REQ-18A clean startMustMy assumption

    We pick a month, stop typing from then, and nothing is billed twice.

    The rule. Nothing dated before the chosen start month is created. A contract already running at go-live starts its schedule from the start month, not from its original start date. Every existing Xero customer is linked to a Salesforce account first, with the doubles resolved. A full rehearsal in the sandbox against the Xero demo company passes before go-live.

    • Given a start month of February 2027 when an invoice dated 28 January 2027 is considered then it is not created.
    • Given a 12 month contract that started on 15 November 2026 and a start month of February 2027 when its schedule is loaded then the first draft is dated 15 February 2027 and the remaining months follow the term.

    From To confirm: G-09, G-12Tested by TC-21, TC-22, TC-24

  19. REQ-19Nothing goes missingMustSaid on the call

    Nothing is lost silently: every won deal or completed job is accounted for each day.

    The rule. Every deal won and every job completed that day ends up as a draft invoice, a scheduled invoice or an item on the failures list. A daily count reconciles them, and anything in none of those places is listed as missing.

    • Given 3 deals were won today and 2 of them became drafts and 1 is on the failures list when the daily count runs then all 3 are accounted for and nothing is listed as missing.
    • Given a won deal that is in none of those places when the daily count runs then it is listed as missing for the finance manager.

    From Tested by TC-23

  20. REQ-20Month end closes with every invoice approvedShouldMy assumption

    The books close on the third working day, so finance is warned if any of the month's invoices is still waiting for approval the working day before.

    The rule. On the working day before the close (the third working day after month end), and on the close itself, any invoice dated in the month that is still a draft or awaiting approval is listed and finance and the named deputy are told. After the close, the same list is marked late.

    • Given an invoice dated 15 January 2027 that is still a draft when it is 2 February 2027 then the close is 3 February 2027 and a warning names the invoice.
    • Given all of January's invoices are approved when it is 2 February 2027 then no warning is raised.

    From To confirm: G-18Tested by TC-25

Two things I would raise with you, not with the client yet

Switch on Xero's own reminders first. The finance manager spends Monday mornings chasing by hand and did not know Xero could do it. Xero has built-in invoice reminders (reported to fire only for invoices marked as sent), so before anything is built for chasing, it is worth switching them on and seeing what is left. [S11]

Repairs look like jobs, not deals. The hardest part of this project is that a repair is invoiced on completion for a final amount, which is a job rather than a sales stage. If the client's work grows, Field Service might suit them. That is a conversation for you and the client, not a suggestion I would make on a first call.

Appendix

The review, and the kit

The part of the work that decides whether a requirement is any good is the review. This shows a first draft of the requirements from the same call next to the set I would send, and the kit that runs the checks.

The working method in the kit: four stages, and where a person stops the line

  1. Draft the requirements from the transcript. An assistant drafts. A person checks every requirement against the lines of the call and removes anything the client did not say.
  2. List what the call left open. The assistant proposes questions. A person decides which matter and writes the suggestion for each.
  3. Write the checks in the client's words. The assistant drafts the steps. A person writes the expected result by hand, from the rules in plain words, before anything runs.
  4. Run the paperwork check and the rule checker. A command reads the case, flags loose words, unsourced requirements and untested ones, runs every check, and stops if anything fails. Nothing goes to the client until it passes and a person has read it.

What the draft did well

  • It stayed on the transcript. Almost every requirement carries an "unclear or assumption" note, and it said that the finance manager and Owen look like one person but the call does not say so.
  • It left the tangents (vans, the office move) out and put the euro customer in a "discussed but not carried forward" list.
  • It caught the start date being in the opportunity notes, the managing director's "nothing goes missing" test, how a running contract joins at go-live, and whether 600 invoices really need approving one at a time. I took the last three.
  • It was closer to the call than my first pass on two points. It made a missing purchase order for a housing association a Must, because the client said they send the invoice straight back, and it kept the third-working-day close. I had dropped both, and have put them back (REQ-07 and REQ-20). Its key for a monthly invoice, one per billing month, was also the right size.

What I changed, and why

  • Priorities. It marked 21 of 28 as Must, and said nobody had ranked them. I kept Must for what the client said must not fail (approval, no double billing, VAT, the failures list) and made timing and deposits Should.
  • Scope. It turned two things nobody asked for into requirements: a job type tracking category (it said so itself) and automatic overdue reminders, which the managing director only said would be "worth something". I moved reminders out, to the section above.
  • Placeholders. 11 of its 60 acceptance criteria say "[monthly amount]" or similar, which cannot be run. Mine use real figures, and real figures forced rules the draft never reached: a deposit of 1,001.01 at half is 500.51 and 500.50, a contract starting on the 31st has no 31st in February, and the April rise compounds.
  • Xero detail. It sensibly held the non-standard VAT cases for the accountant, but never got as far as the Xero tax types, where zero rated, exempt and no VAT are three different things. It asked for part paid to show, but not why that has to be worked out from the amounts: Xero shows an invoice as approved and awaiting payment even when part of it has been paid.
  • Retries. It stopped a second invoice for the same month, but not a retry after a timeout when Xero already holds the invoice. Mine gives every invoice its own code (the job, then the month, the deposit or the balance), writes it in the Reference, and looks in Xero for exactly that code before creating anything.
  • "Quick". It took the managing director's "before the end of the day, ideally" as a soft target. I made it something a check can fail without changing what the managing director asked for: a draft by the end of the working day, and a question on whether something tighter is meant.
