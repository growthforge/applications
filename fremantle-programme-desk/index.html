A pack for the AI Productivity Coordinator

The coordinator's desk, working.

Your advert names four things that make AI adoption work. This pack runs each of them: a register, a chase queue, two forums, a champions network and an impact view, with illustrative data you can click through.

This is a proposal in working form, not past work. Every name, date and figure is illustrative, and none of it comes from Fremantle. The agendas, minutes and chasing are how I would run them.

The advert's own thesis

Four conditions, four mechanisms.

The advert says the organisations getting value from AI are the ones that do these four things. Each one is a working part of the desk below.

"redesign workflows"

A line cannot reach Build until today's workflow and the redesigned one are both written down. If it moves anyway, the desk allows it and flags it.

Where this lives in practice

A page cannot be the source of truth. Your tools are.

The desk shows the rules working. The record itself belongs in the places people already use, and the workbook is the portable copy.

The register: a SharePoint list

One row per workflow, with the columns below, a view for each forum and a view for the staff page. Choice columns hold the stages, status and data class so that nobody types them.

The actions: the same list, or Planner

One row per action with a named owner, a date and a state: open, blocked, done or dropped. Each forum has a view. Planner works if that is what the Lead prefers.

The updates: one Teams channel

One channel for the whole programme, with a pinned post that says what is live and who to ask. The fortnightly post goes in the same channel.

The workbook: the portable copy

The same register, actions, champions, check-ins and impact, with formulas, filters and charts, for anyone who would rather work in Excel or Google Sheets.

The register columns

Title; type; unit; owner (a person); stage; status; data class; sign-off date; budget line; current workflow; redesigned workflow; baseline minutes; minutes with the tool, including review; runs a month; share of output unused; how the estimate was made; where the usage data comes from; who exports it; last update; next milestone; next milestone date.

The Friday chase digest as a Power Automate flow
  1. Recurrence: every Friday, half past eight, UK time.
  2. Get the open items from the actions list.
  3. For each item work out its state from the due date, skipping weekends and bank holidays.
  4. Group by owner, so each person gets one message.
  5. Create a draft in the coordinator's mailbox for each person. Senior owners are left out and listed in one note to the Lead.
  6. Post one summary to the coordinator in Teams.
  7. The coordinator reads, edits and sends. The flow never messages a colleague by itself.

The same rules, written as tested code, are what the desk above runs.

Method and sources

What is real, what is illustrative.

Illustrative. Every person, unit, tool, date, count and hour in the desk, the workbook and the pack. The unit names are invented. Nothing comes from any employer.

Public. Only the points below, each with its source.

  • The four conditions are quoted from the advert for the AI Productivity Coordinator. [S1]
  • Fremantle created a group role for AI in 2025, reporting to its Chief Legal Officer, to oversee adoption and responsible deployment. [S2]
  • Hours are shown gross and net. A cross-government trial reported an average of 26 minutes a day saved, estimated by the participants themselves. [S4] A department's own evaluation of a similar pilot found no evidence that time saved became better productivity, and advised reporting savings adjusted for unused output. [S5]
  • Champions need structure. A maturity model describes champions with no structure or accountability at its first level, and asks for committed hours and the manager's acceptance at the next. It also warns against counting licences and prompts. [S6]
  • The data class and sign-off fields follow the idea in a broadcaster's guidelines for producers and in the producers' trade body principles: keep records of AI use, and do not put confidential or rights-sensitive material into external systems. [S7] [S8] The rule text on the front door is a placeholder for the group's own policy.
  • The look follows the public register of the employer's own site: a near-black canvas, bold sentence-case headings, one magenta accent. No logo, wordmark or image is used. [S9]
  • Type is Figtree, an open-licence typeface, standing in for the proprietary one. [S10]

Method for hours saved: runs a month multiplied by the minutes saved each time gives the gross hours. The time with the tool includes reviewing and correcting the output. Net takes off two things: the saving on output that was not used, and the minutes spent on those attempts before they were thrown away, because that work is then done by hand. Where the minutes on a discarded attempt are not recorded, the desk assumes they took as long as a used output. Hours saved are not money.
