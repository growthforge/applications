# Week 1 kick-off run sheet and the team leader’s “Model it” briefing

> **Illustrative.** A sample run sheet written for an independent job application. Not Vodafone material; not produced, reviewed or endorsed by Vodafone. The customer and the notes are fictional. Names of owners are roles to be agreed, not people.

Companion page: https://growthforge.github.io/applications/vodafone-ai-pathway/ (section “The pathway”).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Pathway | AI pathway for account managers, stage one (week 1) |
| Format | Live cohort, in a room or on a video call; the same run sheet for both |
| Length | 45 minutes |
| Group | One cohort of up to 40 account managers, in breakouts of five |
| Facilitator | The AI Enablement & Academy Specialist |
| Co-presenter | A data protection colleague, for minutes 5 to 15 |
| Helpers | One Copilot champion per breakout (to be agreed with the champion network) |
| Outcome | Every learner leaves with one prompt written for a real meeting this week, and knows what stays out of it |

## Before the session

- [ ] Team leaders have had their briefing (the second half of this file) before week 1.
- [ ] The fictional Larchmoor Freight notes, the M3 prompt card and three data examples are shared in advance, with alternative text and a plain-text copy.
- [ ] Captions on for the video call; slides readable at 200% zoom; no task depends on colour alone.
- [ ] Breakout groups of five set up, each with a champion who has run the four-part prompt on the fictional notes.
- [ ] The approved tool is open for everyone; nobody needs customer data in the session.

## Run sheet

| Time | What happens | Facilitator moves | Script line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Why this pathway: a follow-up that promised a delivery date nobody agreed reached the customer’s finance director | Ask who has had to correct a message like it. Name the rule the twelve weeks teach. | “The tool did not lie on purpose: it completed the pattern. The check is yours.” |
| 5 to 15 min | What stays out, in plain words, co-presented with a data protection colleague | Show three examples: a contract clause, a price list, a public news story. The room sorts each into “approved tools only”, “fine anywhere” or “never”, by poll or show of hands. The co-presenter explains the one the room got wrong. | “If you would not paste it into an email to a stranger, it stays in approved tools.” |
| 15 to 30 min | A first prompt together, then each person writes one for their next real meeting | Build the four parts live on the fictional notes: goal, context, source, expectations. In breakouts, each person writes a prompt for a meeting they have this week; champions check the source line. | “The source line is the one that stops the draft inventing.” |
| 30 to 40 min | Check before you send, in pairs | Partners swap drafts made from the fictional notes and underline every commitment, then find each one in the notes. Collect one draft that needs a fix and one that needs escalating for the whole room. | “Fix what your notes can put right; escalate what only someone else can decide.” |
| 40 to 45 min | Commit | Each person names, in the chat or on a card, the real meeting they will brief with M1 this week. Close with the practice slot and the stage one check. | “Next week your team leader will ask one question: which name, date or figure did you check?” |

### Online variant

- Breakout rooms of five, each with a champion as host. Polls replace the show of hands.
- The facilitator shares one screen only; learners keep the card open beside their own window.
- Put the commit question in the chat and copy the answers into the cohort’s follow-up note.

### If time runs short

Keep minutes 15 to 40 whole. Shorten the opening to two minutes and move the data examples into M2, which every learner takes in week 2.

## Follow-up task (by the week 1 team meeting)

1. Complete M1, “Brief me before the meeting” (20 minutes).
2. Use it to brief yourself for one real customer meeting, in an approved tool.
3. Check every name, date and figure in the brief against its source, and bring one you corrected to the team meeting.

## Team leader briefing: “Model it” in week 1

A 15-minute briefing for team leaders before week 1, so they can open their next team meeting with the pathway.

### What we ask of team leaders across the twelve weeks

| When | Ask |
|---|---|
| Before week 1 | Take this briefing; book the 15-minute practice slot into the team meeting for weeks 3 to 9. |
| Week 1 | Model it: show one prompt of your own, with its check. |
| Week 3 | Set the standard for “checked”: agree with the team what a checked follow-up looks like. |
| Weeks 3 to 9 | Protect the practice slot; ask the one question each week. |
| Week 12 | Confirm each learner’s work sample from real work. |

### The week 1 team meeting, first 10 minutes

| Time | Team leader does | Says |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Shares their own prompt for a real meeting this week, written with the card | “Here is mine. Goal, context, source, expectations.” |
| 3 to 6 min | Shows the draft and the one thing they had to correct | “It got the renewal date wrong. The notes had it right. That is the check.” |
| 6 to 10 min | Asks two or three people for the name, date or figure they corrected in their M1 brief | “Which one did you check?” |

### Do and avoid

- Do show a real correction; a perfect draft teaches nothing.
- Do keep customer data in approved tools when you share your screen.
- Avoid scoring or ranking people in the team meeting; the stage checks do that, privately.
- Avoid turning the slot into a tool demo; one prompt, one check, one question.

## What the session feeds

- **Experience:** “Was this useful for a task you do?” at the end of the session; “Did you use it at work this week?” in the week 1 team meeting (“Module usefulness for the job” and “Weekly use at work” in the metric dictionary).
- **Quality:** the stage one check in week 2 (six decision items).
- Attendance is recorded but never reported as impact.

## Adapting for a market

Keep the timings, the rule and the check. Swap the fictional notes, currency and date formats for the market, run the session in the language the notes are in, and follow the market notes on the M3 card for language support.
