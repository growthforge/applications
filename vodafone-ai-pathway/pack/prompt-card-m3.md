## Task

Turn your meeting notes into a follow-up the customer can act on.

## Prompt

Support fades across the pathway: stage one uses the worked example, stage two the guided template, stage three your own prompt. M3 is a stage two module, so start with **Guided**.

### Worked example

- **Goal:** Draft a follow-up email to the Operations Director at Larchmoor Freight after today’s meeting.
- **Context:** I am their account manager. They want to renew their mobile plan and asked about trailer tracking.
- **Source:** use only my notes, ‘Larchmoor Freight notes, Tuesday’.
- **Expectations:** under 150 words, plain British English, three bullet points, one next step offering the specialist’s two times from the notes, and a list of anything you were unsure about.

### Guided

- **Goal:** Draft a [type of message] to [who] about [what].
- **Context:** I am [your role]; they want [their aim].
- **Source:** use only [the file or thread].
- **Expectations:** [length], [tone], [structure], and a list of anything you were unsure about.

The four parts follow Microsoft’s prompt guidance. Only the goal is required, but the source line is what stops the draft inventing.

### On your own

Write the prompt yourself. It passes if it:

1. names the goal;
2. names the only source Copilot may use;
3. says what a good answer looks like;
4. asks Copilot to list what it was unsure about.

## Check before you send

- [ ] Every commitment appears in the notes or the contract.
- [ ] Names, dates and figures match the source.
- [ ] Nothing from another customer appears.
- [ ] The tone is one you would sign.

## Stop and escalate when

Putting the draft right needs a decision you do not own: price, contract terms, where data is held, or security. Do not send. Ask the owner, and tell the customer when to expect an answer.

**The rule in one line:** fix what your notes can put right; escalate what only someone else can decide.

**Open questions:** a question you said you would check is yours to route. Send it to its owner the same day, whatever happens to the draft.

## Data rule

In line with Vodafone’s published approach of limiting public AI tools, customer contracts and pricing are used only in approved company tools.

## A good and a flawed draft

- **Good:** “...you would like to know more about tracking for your 25 trailers. I will confirm where tracking data is stored.”
- **Flawed:** “...tracking for your 25 trailers, which we will include at no extra cost.” Commits to an offer nobody made.

## Market notes (illustrative markets)

The academy’s own languages are not public; these three show what a market note changes. The structure of the card never changes.

| Market | Note |
|---|---|
| UK | Currency GBP. Dates as 3 March 2027. Ask for plain British English in the expectations. |
| Germany | Currency EUR. Dates as 03.03.2027. Formal address (Sie) for customer emails. Keep the notes in German and ask for German output, so the source and the draft match. German is on Microsoft’s supported-language list. |
| Kenya | Currency KES. Dates as 3 March 2027. Microsoft’s language list shows Swahili for voice on personal accounts only, not for text on work accounts, so the card and its checks stay in English, and a fluent colleague checks any customer message in Swahili before it is sent. |

## Tool notes

Microsoft 365 Copilot in Outlook or Word: name the notes file in the Source line so the source is explicit. Other approved assistants: the same four parts apply; only the way you attach the source changes.

## Why each field is there

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Task and role | Learning starts from the job. |
| Four-part prompt | It matches Microsoft’s own prompt guidance (goal, context, source, expectations). |
| Good and flawed draft | A non-example teaches the check better than a rule. |
| Check | Verification belongs at the point of use. |
| Stop and escalate | The learner needs a named route for decisions they do not own. |
| Data rule | It carries Vodafone’s published guardrails into the moment of work. |
| Owner, version and review date | Feature names and guidance change, so every card expires. |

## Card review (before a card goes into the team library)

- [ ] Owner, version and review date filled in.
- [ ] Task names one real, frequent task for one role.
- [ ] Prompt in four parts, with a source line.
- [ ] A good and a flawed example from realistic, fictional material.
- [ ] Check, escalate rule and data rule present and specific to the task.
- [ ] Tested on three real cases by the contributor; what changed is recorded.
- [ ] Market note added for each market that will use it.
